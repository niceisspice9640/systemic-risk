The constant beneath the comparison

The eye goes to the before-and-after split, but the sturdier finding is the column that's filled every single time: every incident, no matter the year or how far it spread, ran through the information-technology-and-cloud layer. That's not a coincidence — it's the shared substrate every other industry now sits on top of, and it ties this measure straight back to the concentration trend before it.

Read the widening reach carefully. The rise leans on two unusually broad recent incidents — MOVEit and CrowdStrike — and both trace back to a zero-day flaw and a faulty update, not to any AI technique. The mid-2023 line marks when the widest cascades show up, not a cause that produced them. And the coding is deliberately conservative: a cell is filled only where public reporting confirms real impact, so sparse rows like Log4j or Okta reflect high cascade potential with limited documented realized impact — a gap the yes-or-no coding keeps visible on purpose.
